# Local sample provenance

This library contains synthetic fixtures and public sandbox values, not harvested personal data. It is a regression corpus for local matching, not a list of actual people's details. Sources were checked on 4 October 2026. Regeneration is offline and deterministic: `node scripts/generate-samples.mjs`.

The generated positive corpus is created locally during `npm ci` and included in browser/source packages. Git tracks the recipes, seeds and checksum manifest rather than the complete invented credential strings. This prevents synthetic API-key examples from triggering repository secret protection while keeping all detection examples available offline.

The generator combines reserved numbers and sandbox values with different formatting, punctuation, field labels, repeated occurrences, and record contexts. Generated record counts are not counts of independently researched formats and do not establish production detection accuracy. The manifest reports both record counts and unique sensitive values. Numbers are never called; email is never sent; credentials are never authenticated.

API-key shapes, passwords, bearer strings, unsigned example JWTs, and PEM delimiters are generated locally. They are deliberately fake and contain test markers. A shape match does not make a credential active. PEM bodies are not valid cryptographic keys. Contextual identifiers with `TEST` or impossible leading groups, invented names ending in `Testerton`/`Fixtureton`/`Examplevich`, and addresses in invented towns are synthetic. Contextual ID fixtures test conservative handling of sensitive labels; they do not claim validity under national issuance rules.

The regional corpus adds Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan, Ukraine, Moldova, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan. [ITU national numbering plans](https://www.itu.int/ITU-T/inr/nnp/index.html) and the [ITU numbering-plan repository](https://www.itu.int/oth/T0202.aspx?lang=en&parent=T0202) establish calling codes and national number shapes. Regional telephone fixtures use conspicuous zero prefixes; Tajik fixtures use only an all-zero national placeholder because packaged metadata accepts that country's possible-length mobile shapes broadly. These synthetic shapes are not regulator-reserved numbers, and metadata validity does not establish assignment. No subscriber values were collected.

Regional names, surnames and street names are invented locally. IDs contain `TEST` markers and are protected through localized field labels rather than assertions of national validity. Official references for terminology include [Kazakhstan's IIN guidance](https://www.gov.kz/article/648?lang=ru), [Ukraine's RNOKPP guidance](https://www.tax.gov.ua/fizichnim-osobam/otrimannya-kartki/scho-taku-eestratsiyniy-nom/), [Moldova's government IDNP reference](https://probatiune.gov.md/files/getfile/355), [Kyrgyzstan's government PIN guidance](https://shailoo.gov.kg/media/melis/2023/04/09/dalolb.pdf), [Uzbekistan's JSHSHIR guidance](https://my.gov.uz/en/static/jshshir-for-foreigners), and [Azerbaijan's government FIN documentation](https://e.economy.gov.az/senedler/telim/menshe.pdf). No example personal records from those documents were copied.

The sample library is bundled locally and can be browsed without internet access. The detector uses rules and validators to recognize formats and does not send uploaded text to these sources or need an exact-value lookup against this library. Unknown values sharing a supported format can be detected. Selected unlabeled names use a packaged common-given-name lexicon followed by a capitalized surname; selected German and regional addresses require a street marker and house number. These bounded heuristics can miss unfamiliar names and address structures or mask ordinary text sharing those patterns. Unsupported languages, unusual document layouts and arbitrary sensitive narrative still need additional protection.
